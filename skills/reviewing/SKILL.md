# Reviewing

Reviewing decides whether a repair and review loop continues. A reviewer, whoever or whatever it is, produces findings against candidate work, and repairing them is someone else's work. Reviewing judges each finding against the responsibility under review and against what earlier rounds established, and says whether another round is justified or why the loop stops. It runs no reviewer, makes no repair, approves nothing and keeps no record of its own.

The responsibility is what the work under review claims to hold, as the work states it, with the boundary it states. Judge against that statement. Never enlarge it: that a property would be desirable does not make the work owe it. Never shrink it either: a claim is withdrawn only by whatever decides the responsibility, never on the way to a stop.

One rule governs the loop: another round is justified only while it can materially increase confidence that the work holds its responsibility, without changing that responsibility. The questions below make that rule answerable.

First ask what each finding does to the responsibility. It falsifies it when it shows something the work claims not holding. It blocks it when it shows a failure the work did not introduce and does not own that makes the evidence for the work's claim impossible to obtain while it stands: the claim is not shown false, but it cannot be shown true. Otherwise it is outside: it may be real and severe, even caused close by, but either holding it is not what the work claims, or the failure belongs to another owner and the work's evidence can still be obtained despite it. A finding that asks for a property beyond the work's stated boundary is outside, even where the work could be extended to hold it. A finding not yet shown is shown first, by reproducing it; one that cannot be shown falsifies and blocks nothing. Whether a finding is valid, whose it is and what it does to the responsibility are separate questions. Severity, a reviewer's confidence and who found it are not evidence of effect: a severe failure may be outside, and a minor-looking one may falsify the central claim.

Dispose of an outside finding where it belongs, with its owner or where the using system keeps observed failures and possibilities, naming what already records it if something does. Do not repair it as part of this work, and do not let it justify another round.

Then ask of each falsifying finding whether a bounded repair exists. A repair is bounded when it lies inside the responsibility, changing what the work does and not what it claims, and when it closes the whole class the finding is an instance of by whatever decides membership of that class, so that the next review can test it: any further member is either covered or directly falsifies the repair. A repair that lists the members seen so far is not bounded when the class is open, since review can only ever find one more. A bounded repair may state a limit that whatever the work relies on imposes and that no implementation inside the responsibility could overcome, such as what a platform cannot guarantee; that withdraws nothing the work could hold. Stating as a limit something the work could hold is withdrawing a claim.

What earlier rounds established is evidence. Every disposition, whether a class repaired, a finding outside and why, a failure blocking and whose, or a limit stated, is a claim, stated so a later finding could falsify it and kept with the work, where the using system keeps the reasoning behind it. A finding that is a further member of a class a disposition claimed closed falsifies that disposition as well as the work: the repair behind it did not decide the class, and a repair of the same kind is no longer bounded. Only one that decides the class by what actually decides it is. When that is not available within the responsibility, because the class is decided by another owner, a tool, a platform or an environment the work cannot consult or state, no bounded repair exists.

Neither the number of rounds nor that a finding was introduced by an earlier repair is evidence by itself. A repair can introduce a new defect that has a bounded repair of its own, and a new falsifying finding with a bounded repair justifies another round after many rounds as it did after the first. What stops the loop is the absence of a bounded repair, not the length of the loop.

Then decide the loop from all the findings of the round, in this order:

- Reconsider, when some finding falsifies the responsibility and has no bounded repair. Another round against this responsibility cannot converge; the responsibility, its claim, its boundary or the approach that carries it, must change first, and repairing other findings against it gains nothing meanwhile. How it changes is for whatever decides the responsibility. Once it has changed, review begins again against the new statement.
- Continue, when some falsifying finding has a bounded repair. Repair every such finding, state each disposition, and review the repaired work again: a repair is never its own confirmation.
- Blocked, when some finding blocks. Nothing inside the responsibility can establish its claim until the blocking failure is repaired by its owner, so the loop stops without absorbing that repair, and resumes when it is made.
- Ready, when no finding falsifies or blocks. The work holds its responsibility as bounded, as far as review has tested it. Outside findings, disposed of where they belong, may remain. A clean review is one way to be ready, a review whose every finding is outside another; either way the review must have seen the work as it stands after its last repair.

Every loop ends in one of these; there is no undecided. Where it cannot be told whether a finding falsifies the responsibility, the responsibility's statement is ambiguous about it, which is itself a reason to reconsider. Whoever decides the work may always overrule a verdict, but that is a decision made from outside Reviewing, not its way of resolving doubt.
