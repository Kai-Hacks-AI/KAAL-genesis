---
idea: KAAL could need an account of what may end adversarial review of a
  candidate, and of how patterns in review findings should affect the way the
  candidate is being repaired or proven, remembered as Review, a problem not yet
  a capability
---

The possibility is that Review exists in KAAL as a problem of its own, not yet as a capability. KAAL has no account of what may end adversarial review of a candidate, nor of how patterns in review findings should affect the way the candidate is being repaired or proven.

The evidence is pull request #62, at the time of recording still open after fourteen rounds of review by Codex, with architectural guidance from its owner. What it suggests is not only that review took many rounds. Later findings increasingly falsified mechanisms introduced by earlier repairs, rather than independently attacking the original candidate, though not as a strict trend: some late findings still reached older code. The clearest sequence concerns reading a source. Repairs that enumerated the encountered spellings of a dynamic import admitted the spelling just found and were falsified by another member of the same class, four times. Repairs became stronger when the class was instead shaped by its authority and unknown cases received a conservative verdict; stronger is read from what was not reopened, not measured, and a repair shaped by its authority could still fail through how that authority was set up. Centralising the reading in a single reader did not by itself settle it: minutes after a repair made one reading authoritative, review found that reading incomplete, and one incomplete interpretation stayed incomplete when it was the only one.

Read that way, repeated adversarial review can produce evidence about three different things: about the candidate, when an obligation it owes is falsified; about a repair mechanism, when a repair introduced to answer review is itself falsified again and again; and about a disposition, when later evidence shows that the reason an earlier finding was considered dealt with does not hold. The second matters particularly. Once review keeps falsifying the repairs made in answer to review, the evidence may no longer say only that there is another defect. It may say that the way the candidate is being repaired or proven is not converging. In #62 that transition was recognised by the owner, observing from outside the repair loop, not by anything KAAL has. Review may therefore need a boundary, evidence, an escalation signal and a termination condition of its own.

A related hypothesis, not a division of responsibility: Testing determines how proof should learn from a finding; Review may determine when the pattern of findings is itself evidence that the current proof or repair mechanism needs reconsidering. The lesson about learning from a finding belongs to Testing and is not repeated here: since #70, landed while #62 was open and learned substantially from it, Testing says that a finding names an instance, that the missed class is derived from its authority, that every consumer is reached and that what remains unknown is stated.

About termination, #62 exposes that KAAL has no endogenous account of when adversarial review may stop. Neither a number of rounds, elapsed time, the severity of findings, agreement among reviewers, a clean review from one reviewer, nor suppressing later findings because they resemble earlier ones answers that. A later counterexample must stay able to falsify a claimed repair. So the tempting rule that another member of an already-disposed class never reopens review is false where the disposition claimed that the class was repaired: another member is then evidence that the claim was false. A disposition that explicitly gives something up, or assigns it elsewhere, claims something different, and another instance does not necessarily falsify it. This is an observation about what dispositions claim, not a set of states.

An eventual stop condition would say when stopping is justified, not ensure that stopping eventually occurs. It must permit non-termination: where review has demonstrated an unsoundness that is neither repaired nor authoritatively removed from what the candidate owes, refusal or continued unresolved review may be the right result.

The counterevidence and opposite pressures are part of the record. Review must not manufacture convergence either: in #62 a blanket rule to leave unresolved whatever could not be shown was withdrawn once it would have refused ordinary changes, so a candidate can become trivially conservative, refusing or leaving unresolved whatever it cannot prove, which protects soundness while destroying the capability it was meant to provide. Review might surface that tension, but which concept owns it is not decided. #62 shows review falsifying obligations the candidate already had, not Review legitimately enlarging them, so nothing here lets Review create an obligation because it notices something desirable. In #62 repairs were answered as fixed as a class, and those class claims were then falsified. Where the same authority that produces a repair can declare its finding disposed, any stop condition can collapse into declaration, which suggests that Review needs some independence between evidence and disposition; the evidence does not support defining that independence as another model, another agent, human review or agreement among reviewers. And KAAL is not without a response to recurrence: Testing already says that when the same kind of failure keeps being found, the fix changes the mechanism that lets it happen, and architecting already works backward from review findings that recur. Neither says who recognises that a repair made in answer to review has itself become what review keeps falsifying, or whom that is surfaced to, which is the gap this records, but both may turn out to be enough.

Review may compose with what KAAL already has: Requirement, Feature, Regression and Acceptance for what a candidate owes; Defect for an observed failure; Case and Run for evidence; Suite for a shared concern; Acceptance for loss deliberately given up; and ownership, as architecting decides it, for decisions Review cannot make itself. Several responsibilities have no clear owner: what sets the floor of what a candidate ought to promise, what owns the extent of a failure class, what limits a new promise, who may dispose a finding, and what reviewer independence means. That does not show that a new concept is needed. No new concept is justified yet; several responsibilities remain unresolved.

This relates to `ideas/how-are-we-doing/idea.md`, whose ARE, Agents Review Each-other, retains the usefulness of independently tasked review, and to `ideas/addiction/idea.md`, which also takes #62 as its evidence. It leaves both as recorded.

It is not yet known what identifies a finding class, who may dispose a finding, what independence Review requires, whether a clean review is meaningful evidence, or whether Review is itself a skill. Further evidence is expected from #62 itself, which continues under the Testing that learned from it. Whether its review changes character afterwards is left to be observed, and nothing here predicts it.

This record keeps the possibility only. It births no Review capability or skill and defines no finding identities, finding classes, disposition states, review ledgers, escalation counters, stop predicates, reviewer roles, Review Runs or Review Plans. It is not a Requirement or a commitment, and it changes nothing in #62, testing, Run, Acceptance, FAR, Regression, seals, releases, Genesis or BRAIN.
