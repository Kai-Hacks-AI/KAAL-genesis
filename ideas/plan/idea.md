---
idea: KAAL's planning could be one loop of deciding what deserves priority, the
  intended capability order and dependencies, how Now adapts toward Next, and
  planning becoming current action, remembered provisionally as PLAN —
  Prioritize, Launch, Adaption, Now
---

The mnemonic and its current working expansion are:

- P: Prioritize, which is Backlog, what deserves consideration and priority;
- L: Launch, which is Roadmap, the intended progression of capability and its dependency order;
- A: Adaption, which is Change, how Now adapts toward Next;
- N: Now, which is planning becoming current action.

The wording is provisional. The meaning matters more than the acronym, and the acronym should not be completed or made grammatical by distorting what it names. None of these four is a KAAL capability or committed architecture, and this record does not birth Planning, Backlog, Roadmap or Change.

The evidence is how KAAL's Testing was built. The roadmap used while building it repeatedly did real work: it drove critical-path order, showed which work could proceed in parallel, justified deferring what was not yet needed, and was revised when new evidence arrived. That is the pattern this Idea retains. A statement of what deserves consideration, an intended order of capabilities that respects their dependencies, a way for the present to adapt toward what comes next, and the moment at which planning becomes the action actually being taken, together seem to make one loop rather than four separate lists. The roadmap in that work was the part that visibly changed decisions, but it did so because the rest was implicitly there: something had to be worth considering, and the present had to be able to move.

The four working meanings are kept as possibilities. Prioritize suggests that what deserves consideration and what deserves priority may be one concern, a Backlog understood as consideration ordered by worth rather than as a pile. Launch suggests a Roadmap as intended capability progression and dependency order, which is not a schedule, and is what made critical path, parallelism and deferral readable. Adaption suggests that Change is how Now adapts toward Next, in the sense already meant in `ideas/we-can-go-far/idea.md`, where Now, Change and Next were observed and Backlog and Roadmap appeared beside Change only as context. Now suggests that planning becoming current action is where a plan stops being intention and starts being work, and where evidence to revise it begins.

This is related to `ideas/we-can-go-far/idea.md`, which it leaves as recorded. There, Backlog (what?) and Roadmap (when?) were named only as context beside Change, and CAN, Changes Adaption Now, gestured at the same Now, Change, Next. This Idea does not settle that ambiguity or complete that one. It takes the planning vocabulary that Idea left unowned and asks whether it may hold together as one loop, while leaving both records as they are.

It should also be kept apart from what KAAL already calls a Plan. In testing, a Plan is runnable, a composition carried by Suites, and the Feature, Acceptance and Regression Test Plans are testing's own. PLAN here is a provisional mnemonic about how work is prioritized, ordered and adapted, not a Test Plan, and nothing here changes what a Test Plan means. Whether the two are ever related, and whether the name survives that collision, is left open.

The evidence is KAAL's own development, in one line of work, with no controlled or external validation. Further evidence is expected from the work that follows testing: whether the same roadmap behaviour recurs outside it, whether critical-path order, parallelism, deferral and revision remain readable without machinery, and whether anything beyond a roadmap turns out to have been doing the work. If it does not recur, this possibility should be revised or abandoned in whatever way KAAL then has for acting on an Idea, not treated as proven.

This record keeps the possibility only. It does not commit KAAL to a planning, backlog, roadmap or change capability, to any of their records, states, priorities, schedules or formats, to workflow machinery, to tooling, or to the current expansion of the mnemonic. It is not a Requirement or a roadmap commitment, and it changes nothing in testing, FAR, Regression, seals, releases, Genesis or BRAIN.
