id: rat
idea: "RAT, Requirements Architecture Testing, retained as a possible triangle
  of forces: each pushes questions on the other two, and structure is evidence
  for those questions, not an automatic rule to split."
---

RAT stands for Requirements, Architecture, Testing. The possibility is that the three form a triangle of forces whose pressure runs in every direction. It is not a workflow, a sequence or an order of work.

From Requirements: when more than one independently necessary protection or evidence boundary presses on a Requirement, it is fair to ask whether it is really more than one Requirement. From Testing: when one test structure is asked to establish more than one Requirement or semantic claim, it is fair to ask whether the test structure should split. From Architecture: the structure of the solution and of delivery constrains, and is constrained by, both, so when a Requirement boundary or a Test boundary does not fit the architecture honestly, that pressure is to be resolved and not hidden.

The point worth keeping is that structure is evidence and questions, not an automatic split rule. A split is earned when the parts turn out to have genuinely different evidence boundaries, and not merely because a structure suggests one.

Provenance: it was discussed as Requirements management and Test management negotiating through their structure, with Architecture as the third force. The worked evidence is FAR-15 (#171): the Requirement that a check is named by what it asserts, and that an environment appears in its name only when the environment is part of what it asserts, was pulled on by Testing. Testing found two clauses with different evidence boundaries, one with accepted KAAL meaning to evidence it and one with none, so the pressure from Testing questioned the Requirement's boundary. That evidence is recorded here as it happened, and #171 decides what it concludes.

This is an Idea only. It is not BRAIN learning, a principle or a process, and it births no Requirement, workflow rule or capability. Further evidence should decide whether KAAL learns it, changes it or leaves it.
