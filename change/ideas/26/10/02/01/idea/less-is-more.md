The mnemonic, kept exactly as it was recorded: LESS IS MORE, Let Experience Support Simple Implementations So More Optimization Reaches Enhancements. The wording is not improved or completed to make the acronym more elegant.

The possibility is not that less code is better, nor that the smallest implementation should be designed first. It is that experience may reveal the real responsibility, so that an implementation can become simpler once it expresses that responsibility, and later optimization then has a clearer target. Complexity met while learning is not automatically failure. That interpretation is provisional.

Provenance: the Idea was first recorded in the historical material of #62, from the worked experience of Protection Evolution there. That record is context only. It is not restored here, and the old Ideas tree is not brought back.

Present evidence, from FAR-15 (#171, #172, #173): reaching for larger semantic machinery came first, and experience then exposed the smaller semantics that a Case or a Suite states what it tests of a Requirement. #172 implemented only what that evidence earned. Using it showed the next concrete gap, that Plan derivation ignored a Suite's claim, which #173 addresses. Meanwhile the work deliberately refused to design Requirement decomposition in advance, or to infer what a check asserts from how GitHub executes it. The simplification was earned by experience, not designed from a preference for less machinery.

This is an Idea only. It is not BRAIN learning and not a KAAL principle, and it births no Requirement, workflow rule or capability. Further evidence should decide whether KAAL learns it, changes it or leaves it.
