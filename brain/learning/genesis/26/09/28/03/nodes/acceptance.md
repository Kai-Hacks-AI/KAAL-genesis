# Acceptance

Acceptance is the accepted regression less what a candidate explicitly gives up of it. KAAL's per-change testing is growing toward Feature, Acceptance and Regression: Feature names what the candidate newly promises; Acceptance keeps everything the accepted regression protects except what the candidate explicitly excludes; Regression will then be what Acceptance keeps, with what Feature newly promised and demonstrated. This node records only Acceptance.

The Acceptance Test Plan is the accepted Regression Plan, but not these inherited cases and suites. The accepted regression already embodies the testing KAAL protects: its plan's requirements, under its conditions, shown by its own cases. Acceptance requires all of it of the candidate, replayed against the candidate as the accepted regression replays its cases, except what the candidate excludes. It never reads the candidate's own Regression Plan: a candidate cannot give up anything by saying less, so no longer requiring Windows or the seal checks in its own plan changes nothing that is inherited. It owns no model of protection of its own: what it keeps is the accepted plan and its cases, less a named few.

A candidate excludes an inherited case or suite only by naming it, and why, in an acceptance record it adds to `acceptance/`: a case by its file and title in the accepted regression, where its address says which case it is, or a suite that serves the accepted plan. Silence retains: an inherited case that no longer holds against the candidate is refused unless it is excluded, and so is an exclusion of anything the accepted regression does not have. Excluding a suite gives up only that the plan requires it, never its cases, which still show what they help prove. A commitment its cases alone show is given up once every inherited case of it is excluded, and never by less. A record the accepted state already holds is history: it gave up something of an earlier regression, excludes nothing more, and is never rewritten or removed, so a waiver can neither wait for a later loss nor be added again as if new. With no record, the candidate gives up nothing, which it may only while every inherited case holds against it.

Acceptance is a test plan: what it keeps must be shown, and a run shows it. What a run cannot show is whether giving something up is wise. That is the judgement of whoever accepts the candidate; KAAL does not make it, and never infers it from the candidate causing a case to fail. What it does check is that every exclusion is explicit, names what the accepted regression has, is new to this candidate against this accepted state, and says why.

A replacement is two independent decisions. Feature names the successor as a new promise and requires it demonstrated; Acceptance excludes the inherited cases of what it replaces. Neither implies the other. A Requirement moved to another place gives up nothing: its inherited cases still hold, so it stays required beside the new place Feature names, until they are excluded. A Requirement the accepted plan names is never rewritten or removed, even once every case of it is excluded: excluding its cases lets its commitment leave the regression, never its record, which is history.

The next regression can be derived without guessing: what the Acceptance Test Plan still requires, shown by the inherited cases it keeps, with what the candidate newly promised and demonstrated. The regression plan's own record of what it replaces and withdraws is, for the decision to give something up, now the acceptance records' role.

What is left open: only cases and suites can be given up. Conditions and proof other than cases are inherited as they are, since no real change has yet needed to give one up. Nothing yet requires that acceptance hold before a candidate is accepted: the accepted regression's checker still judges candidates as before, until the regression is derived from acceptance.
