# Architecting

KAAL uses the **architecting** skill so that deciding where a responsibility belongs, which side states a relation, and when experience shows such a boundary is wrong is done deliberately, rather than rediscovered in each conversation, implementation round and review.

KAAL had been doing this repeatedly without a capability for it, which is the evidence for moving it forward in BASS, from Bare to Skill, as testing was moved. Forward, from intended responsibility: a case says which suites it belongs to, and a suite which plans it serves, and neither a suite nor a plan lists what is beneath it, because the side whose meaning includes a relation, and whose lifecycle should carry it, states it; a suite's concern and a plan's purpose stay as they are while what carries them is found, split or replaced. A Requirement was given a place of its own because a commitment stated inside something that changes in place cannot be identified apart from it. Backward, from evidence: states, runs and changes were separated from the branches, jobs and pull requests that carried them once those operations turned out to be stating KAAL's meaning. The Acceptance work passed through a model of protection of its own, and then a reuse of what plans require, before finding that the accepted regression already is the protection, and that Acceptance owns only what a candidate deliberately excludes of it: implementation pressure showed duplication, and the responsibility already had an owner. The many review findings that hardened how plans are read, by contrast, moved no owner: not every finding is pressure on a boundary.

No existing capability owned that reasoning. BRAIN holds what KAAL means once it has learned it, not how to find where a meaning should live. An Idea keeps a possibility, a Requirement a commitment, a defect an observed failure, and testing the evidence; a suite can show a concern shared across capabilities, but not whether they share a responsibility. The skill is the reasoning that decides between them. It depends on none of them and says nothing of KAAL, which remains an example of its use.

KAAL keeps no record of architecture. What such a record would hold already has a place: what KAAL learned an owner means belongs in BRAIN, a structure it might adopt in an Idea, what must hold in a Requirement, an intended structure observed failing in a defect, and the reasoning behind one change with that change. Until a conclusion turns up that fits none of them, a record of its own would only duplicate them. Nor does KAAL require an architectural analysis of any change, or judge one.

The backward loop of worked evidence, learning and refactoring that `ideas/we-can-go-far/idea.md` retains as a possibility is where backward architecting would sit: evidence across capabilities showing that an owner should be strengthened, split, merged or reconsidered. Architecting does not make that loop KAAL's, and how a refactoring it proposes is shown to keep what KAAL has accepted is testing's to decide, not decided here.

The skill explains how to architect. This node records why KAAL uses it.
